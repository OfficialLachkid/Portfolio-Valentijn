## Position Overview
During my internship at [Domits](https://www.domits.com/), I did not operate only as a feature developer. Within the engineering team, I took on a lead fullstack developer role with responsibility for technical quality, delivery coordination, and release flow.

That meant combining hands-on fullstack software development with daily leadership of engineers, review ownership, release decisions, and close communication with management. My role sat in the middle of engineering execution: translating product direction into development progress, keeping the team moving, and making sure new work reached the correct quality level before release.

## My Role In Practice
My position combined technical delivery with operational leadership. The role included:

- leading engineers during active feature development
- reviewing pull requests and checking implementation quality
- merging approved work into the `development` branch
- coordinating production-ready merges from `development` into `main`
- leading morning and afternoon standups and standdowns
- tracking work progress and expected delivery time
- helping teammates when they were blocked or needed technical support
- staying in close communication with the CEO about progress, priorities, and release readiness
- monitoring quality assurance when new features were delivered

This made the role broader than writing code alone. It required oversight of both the engineering details and the day-to-day movement of the team.

## Engineering Leadership
A large part of the role was guiding the engineering team through active work. That meant keeping a clear view of who was working on what, how far features had progressed, and where risks or delays were starting to appear.

In practice, I regularly checked:

- whether work was still moving according to expectation
- how long implementation would likely take
- where dependencies or blockers were slowing progress
- which issues needed technical help or a sharper decision

This helped keep momentum high and reduced the chance that developers stayed stuck on a problem for too long without support.

## Review And Merge Responsibility
I also had ownership in the review and merge process. That responsibility mattered because code quality and release stability depend heavily on what gets approved and when.

My work here included:

- checking pull requests for correctness, structure, and maintainability
- identifying issues before code moved further through the branch flow
- merging reviewed work into `development`
- helping manage the transition from `development` to `main` for production releases

This gave me an important role in protecting quality across the codebase, not only within the features I implemented myself.

## Standups, Progress, And Communication
Another important part of my position was daily coordination. I led the standups in the morning and afternoon, as well as standdowns, so the team stayed aligned on the state of the work.

These moments were used to:

- confirm priorities
- track implementation progress
- surface blockers early
- adjust planning where needed
- keep engineering and management aligned

I also communicated closely with the CEO about delivery progress, team status, and whether features were ready to move forward. That meant the role included both technical leadership and reporting responsibility.

## Quality Assurance And Release Readiness
When new features were delivered, quality assurance was part of my responsibility as well. The goal was not only to finish work, but to make sure new functionality was stable enough to move through the release flow.

That required attention to:

- whether a feature behaved correctly
- whether edge cases or regressions had been missed
- whether the implementation was ready for review and merge
- whether the release state was solid enough for production

This quality-oriented part of the role was especially important in a fast-moving product environment where multiple engineers were contributing at the same time.

## Why This Role Matters
This role is one of the strongest examples of my internship because it shows responsibility beyond implementation alone. I was not only contributing code, but also helping lead the engineering process around that code.

The position combined:

- software development
- engineering coordination
- review and merge ownership
- release awareness
- quality control
- direct communication with management

That combination reflects both technical depth and ownership within a real product team.
